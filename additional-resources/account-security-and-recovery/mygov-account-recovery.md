# myGov Account Security

**Think someone has accessed your myGov account?**

Your myGov account can connect you to important Australian Government services, including:

- Australian Taxation Office (ATO).
- Centrelink.
- Medicare.
- Other linked government services.

Unauthorised access could expose personal information or allow someone to change details within linked services.

Take this seriously, but don't panic. There are steps you can take.

## Step 1. Use the official myGov website

Go directly to:

[myGov](https://my.gov.au/)

Do not use links from unexpected text messages, emails or social media posts claiming to be myGov.

If you cannot sign in, use the official help and recovery options.

## Step 2. Check whether someone accessed your account

If you can sign in:

1. Open your myGov account.
2. Review your account history.
3. Check your linked services.
4. Review connected devices.
5. Look for changes you did not make.

Pay particular attention to:

- New linked services.
- Services that have disappeared.
- Changed personal details.
- Changed bank account details.
- Unfamiliar devices or activity.
- Unexpected sign-in methods.

## Step 3. Secure your account

If you still have access:

1. Change your password if you use one.
2. Review your sign-in settings.
3. Remove unfamiliar devices or passkeys.
4. Review your security options.
5. Set up a stronger sign-in method where possible.

myGov supports options including Digital ID and passkeys.

Use the strongest option you can manage.

## Step 4. Check your linked services

Do not assume securing myGov automatically resolves every issue in every connected service.

Check each linked service for suspicious changes.

Pay particular attention to:

### Australian Taxation Office

Look for unexpected changes to:

- Bank account details.
- Tax records.
- Personal information.
- Lodgements or other activity.

### Centrelink

Look for unexpected changes to:

- Contact details.
- Payment details.
- Claims.
- Personal information.

### Medicare

Look for unfamiliar changes to personal information or other activity.

If you find suspicious activity, contact the affected service directly using its official contact details.

## Step 5. Contact the myGov helpdesk

If you cannot secure your account, cannot sign in or believe someone has accessed it, contact the myGov helpdesk through the official website.

[myGov Help](https://my.gov.au/)

Do not rely on phone numbers supplied in suspicious messages or search advertisements.

## Step 6. Contact Services Australia if relevant

If your Centrelink or Medicare information may have been accessed, contact Services Australia through its official website.

[Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/)

If your tax information may have been accessed, contact the ATO.

[Australian Taxation Office](https://www.ato.gov.au/)

## Step 7. Contact your bank if financial details were exposed

If someone changed your bank details, accessed financial information or made suspicious transactions, contact your bank immediately.

Use the official number on your bank card or website.

## Step 8. Get identity support

If personal information or identity documents may have been stolen, contact:

[IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/) – 1800 595 160.

IDCARE provides support for people affected by identity theft and cyber incidents.

## Step 9. Report the incident

- [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report)
- [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/)

Keep copies of suspicious messages, notifications and incident reference numbers.

## Official myGov resources

- [Recover after your device has been hacked](https://my.gov.au/en/services/living-arrangements/experiencing-crime-and-other-risks-online/what-to-do-if-things-go-wrong-online/recover-after-being-hacked)
- [What to do if you've been scammed](https://my.gov.au/en/about/privacy-and-security/security/mygov-scams/what-to-do-if-you-ve-been-scammed)

## Important

Government services will not ask you to share your password or one-time verification codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

If something feels suspicious, stop and contact the service directly.
