# Clicked a Suspicious Link?

**Clicked a dodgy link in an email, text message or social media post?**

First: take a breath.

Clicking a suspicious link does not automatically mean your device has been hacked.

What matters is what happened next.

Did you just open a webpage? Enter a password? Provide bank details? Download a file? Install something?

Follow the section that matches what happened.

## Scenario 1. I clicked the link but didn't do anything else

If you opened a suspicious webpage but did not enter information, download anything or approve a request:

1. Close the webpage.
2. Do not click anything else on it.
3. Do not allow notifications or permissions.
4. Check your downloads folder for anything unexpected.
5. Close any suspicious browser tabs.
6. Report or delete the original message.

You do not automatically need to change every password just because you opened a link.

However, if the page caused unusual behaviour, downloaded something or requested permissions, investigate further.

## Scenario 2. I entered my password

Treat the password as compromised.

Using a trusted device:

1. Open the real website or official application.
2. Change the affected account's password.
3. Sign out other devices or sessions.
4. Review recent account activity.
5. Check recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
6. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
7. Check for unfamiliar connected applications.

If you reused that password on other websites, change it there too.

Start with your email account because it can often be used to reset other passwords.

## Scenario 3. I entered a verification code

A verification code may allow someone to sign in or take control of an account.

Act immediately:

1. Identify which service sent the code.
2. Open that service's official website or application.
3. Change your password if you can.
4. Sign out unfamiliar sessions.
5. Review recovery details.
6. Check multi-factor authentication settings.
7. Contact the service if you cannot regain control.

Never share another verification code with anyone.

## Scenario 4. I entered my bank or card details

Contact your bank immediately.

Do not wait to see whether money disappears.

Tell them:

> I entered my banking or card details into a website that I now believe was fraudulent. I need help securing my account and checking for unauthorised transactions.

Ask your bank whether your card needs to be cancelled or replaced.

If you transferred money, report it immediately and ask whether the transaction can be stopped or recalled.

## Scenario 5. I entered personal or identity information

If you provided information such as:

- Full name.
- Date of birth.
- Address.
- Driver licence details.
- Passport details.
- Medicare details.
- Tax File Number.

Consider contacting IDCARE.

[IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/) – 1800 595 160.

If government information may have been exposed, contact the relevant government service directly.

## Scenario 6. I downloaded a file but didn't open it

Do not open the file.

1. Do not run or install it.
2. Delete it if you are confident it is the suspicious download.
3. Empty the recycle bin or trash if appropriate.
4. Run a security scan using trusted security software.
5. Check for other unexpected downloads.

If you are unsure whether the file has been opened automatically or installed, seek technical assistance.

## Scenario 7. I opened or ran a downloaded file

This is more serious because the file may have executed code.

If you suspect malware:

1. Disconnect the device from Wi-Fi or its network.
2. Do not use it to access banking or important accounts.
3. Do not enter new passwords on that device.
4. Use a separate, trusted device to secure important accounts.
5. Run a full security scan using trusted software.
6. Seek help from a trusted IT professional if you are unsure.

If the device is used for work, contact your IT or security team immediately.

Do not attempt to hide the incident or delete evidence from a work device.

## Scenario 8. I installed an application or gave someone remote access

If you installed an unfamiliar application or allowed someone to control your device:

1. Disconnect the device from the internet if you suspect active compromise.
2. Stop communicating with the person.
3. Do not provide further passwords or codes.
4. Use another trusted device to contact your bank if financial information may be exposed.
5. Seek help from a trusted IT professional.
6. Tell your workplace IT team if it was a work device.

Do not assume uninstalling the application completely removes the threat.

## What if the website asked me to scan a QR code?

QR codes can be used to direct you to phishing websites or link an account to another device.

If you scanned a code:

- Check what website or application opened.
- Do not approve unexpected login requests.
- Review linked devices in the affected service.
- Remove unfamiliar sessions or devices.
- Change your password if you entered it.

For WhatsApp, review Settings > Linked devices.

## What if I gave someone remote access?

Treat this as a potentially serious compromise.

The person may have been able to see information on your screen, access files or interact with applications.

Disconnect the device from the internet if you suspect ongoing access. Use a separate trusted device to secure important accounts and contact your bank if financial information was accessible.

Seek professional help before using the affected device for sensitive activity again.

## Report the incident

Australian support:

- [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report)
- [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/)
- [IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/) – 1800 595 160
- [ACSC Hotline](https://www.cyber.gov.au/) – 1300 292 371

If money has been lost, contact your bank first.

## Remember

You are not the first person to click a suspicious link, and you will not be the last.

Scammers design these messages to look convincing and pressure people into acting quickly.

The important thing is to work out what information or access may have been exposed, then take the right steps.
