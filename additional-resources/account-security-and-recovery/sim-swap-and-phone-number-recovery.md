# SIM Swap & Phone Number Recovery

**Has your phone suddenly lost mobile service for no obvious reason?**

If your phone unexpectedly shows No Service, SOS only or cannot receive calls and text messages, it may be a network issue.

However, it can also be a warning sign that someone has transferred your phone number to another SIM card or mobile provider.

This is sometimes called a SIM swap or number porting fraud.

## Why this matters

Your mobile number may be used to receive:

- Banking verification codes.
- Password reset codes.
- Email recovery messages.
- Social media login codes.
- Identity verification messages.

If someone takes control of your number, they may use it to access other accounts.

## Step 1. Contact your mobile provider immediately

Use another phone if necessary.

Contact your provider using the number on its official website.

Tell them:

> I believe my mobile number may have been transferred or SIM-swapped without my permission. I need you to investigate immediately and secure my number.

Ask them to:

- Check whether your number has been ported to another provider.
- Check whether a SIM replacement was requested.
- Reverse an unauthorised port if one occurred.
- Deactivate an unauthorised SIM.
- Restore your number to a SIM you control.
- Add extra identity verification to your account.
- Secure your provider account against further changes.

Ask for a reference number for the incident.

## Step 2. Contact your bank immediately

Do not wait until your phone service is restored.

Tell your bank that your mobile number may be compromised.

Ask them to:

- Check for unusual transactions.
- Secure your accounts.
- Block suspicious transfers if possible.
- Review recent password or security changes.
- Temporarily restrict risky activity if needed.
- Change your verification method if appropriate.

Use your bank's official phone number or application.

## Step 3. Secure your email account

Your email account is one of the most important accounts to protect.

If possible, sign in from a trusted device and:

1. Change your email password.
2. Remove unfamiliar recovery methods.
3. Review recent sign-ins.
4. Sign out unfamiliar sessions.
5. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
6. Check forwarding rules and inbox filters.

If your mobile number was used for email recovery, review those settings carefully.

## Step 4. Secure other important accounts

Review accounts that use your phone number for recovery or verification.

Prioritise:

- Banking.
- Primary email.
- myGov.
- Apple or Google accounts.
- Password managers.
- Social media.
- Shopping and payment services.

Change passwords if you suspect they have been exposed.

Do not rely on SMS verification while your phone number is still compromised.

## Step 5. Check for suspicious activity

Look for:

- Password reset notifications.
- Unexpected sign-in alerts.
- Banking transactions you do not recognise.
- Changes to account recovery details.
- New devices.
- Messages you did not send.
- Notifications about a new SIM or number transfer.

Keep copies of relevant messages and incident reference numbers.

## Step 6. Protect your mobile provider account

Once your number is restored:

- Change your provider account password.
- Change your account PIN if available.
- Add a porting or number-transfer lock if your provider offers one.
- Ask about additional identity verification.
- Check account contact details.
- Review recent account activity.

Ask your provider what security measures are available to prevent another unauthorised transfer.

## Step 7. Report the incident

In Australia:

- [ACMA – What to do if your mobile number has been stolen](https://www.acma.gov.au/what-do-if-your-mobile-number-has-been-stolen)
- [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report)
- [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/)
- [IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/) – 1800 595 160

The Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) provides guidance on stolen numbers, SIM swaps and unauthorised number transfers.

## What if my phone simply has no service?

A network outage, damaged SIM or device fault can also cause a loss of service.

Contact your provider to investigate. Do not assume it is a SIM swap, but treat unexplained loss of service seriously if you also receive unexpected account or banking notifications.

## Remember

Your phone number is part of your digital identity. If someone takes control of it, act quickly and contact your provider and bank.
