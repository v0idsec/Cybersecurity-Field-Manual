# Gmail & Google Account Recovery

**Think someone has accessed your Gmail or Google account?**

Your Google account may provide access to Gmail, Google Drive, Photos, YouTube, contacts, saved passwords and other personal information.

Start with Google's official recovery process.

## Step 1. Try to recover your account

Use:

- [Google Account Recovery](https://accounts.google.com/signin/recovery)
- [Google Account Security](https://myaccount.google.com/security)
- [Google's hacked account guidance](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825)

Follow the prompts. Google may ask questions to confirm that the account belongs to you.

Use a device and location you normally use to sign in if possible.

## Step 2. Change your password

If you can sign in:

1. Open [Google Account Security](https://myaccount.google.com/security).
2. Select your password settings.
3. Change your password.
4. Choose a unique password that you do not use elsewhere.

If you cannot sign in, continue with account recovery.

## Step 3. Review recent security activity

Visit:

[Google Security Checkup](https://myaccount.google.com/security-checkup)

Review:

- Recent security events.
- Unfamiliar sign-ins.
- Devices you do not recognise.
- New recovery email addresses.
- New recovery phone numbers.
- Unfamiliar authentication methods.

Remove devices and methods you do not recognise.

## Step 4. Sign out unfamiliar devices

Open:

[Your Google Devices](https://myaccount.google.com/device-activity)

Review devices with access to your account.

Sign out devices you do not recognise or no longer use.

## Step 5. Check Gmail settings

An attacker may have changed Gmail settings to monitor your email.

Open [Gmail](https://mail.google.com/).

Check the following:

### Forwarding

Go to Gmail settings and review forwarding.

Make sure messages are not being forwarded to an unfamiliar address.

### Filters

Look for filters that automatically:

- Delete messages.
- Archive messages.
- Forward messages.
- Mark messages as read.
- Hide security notifications.

Remove anything you did not create.

### Delegated access

Check whether another person has been given access to your mailbox.

Remove unfamiliar delegates.

### Sent and deleted messages

Look for messages you did not send or items that have been deleted unexpectedly.

## Step 6. Check connected applications

Open:

[Google Account Third-Party Connections](https://myaccount.google.com/connections)

Review applications and services connected to your account.

Remove anything unfamiliar or unnecessary.

## Step 7. Check other Google services

Review:

- Google Drive and shared files.
- Google Photos and shared albums.
- YouTube channel activity.
- Google Play purchases.
- Saved passwords.
- Contact information.

Look for changes you did not make.

## Step 8. Turn on multi-factor authentication

Use:

[Google Account Security](https://myaccount.google.com/security)

Set up a supported method such as a passkey, authenticator app or security key.

Do not approve sign-in prompts you did not initiate.

## Step 9. Secure other accounts

If you used the same password elsewhere, change those passwords.

Start with banking, social media and other email accounts.

## If you still cannot access your account

Continue using Google's recovery process:

[Google Account Recovery](https://accounts.google.com/signin/recovery)

Google may need time to verify your identity. Avoid paying third parties who claim they can bypass the recovery process.
