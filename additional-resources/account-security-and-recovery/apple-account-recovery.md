# Apple Account Recovery

**Think someone has accessed your Apple Account or iCloud?**

Your Apple Account may provide access to photos, messages, contacts, backups, purchases, devices and other personal information.

Apple now uses the term Apple Account, although you may still see it referred to as an Apple ID.

## Step 1. Try to sign in

Use Apple's official website:

- [Apple Account](https://account.apple.com/)
- [Apple Account Recovery](https://iforgot.apple.com/)
- [Apple Support – Account security](https://support.apple.com/en-au/102560)

If you cannot sign in, follow the recovery instructions.

## Step 2. Change your password

If you can access your account:

1. Visit [account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com/).
2. Sign in.
3. Open the sign-in and security settings.
4. Change your password.
5. Use a unique password that you have not used elsewhere.

You can also change your password from a trusted Apple device.

## Step 3. Review your devices

Check the devices associated with your Apple Account.

On an iPhone or iPad:

1. Open Settings.
2. Tap your name.
3. Scroll through the device list.
4. Select any device you do not recognise.
5. Review its details and remove it if it is not yours.

You can also review your account at [account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com/).

## Step 4. Check trusted phone numbers

Review the trusted phone numbers associated with your account.

Make sure they belong to you or people you deliberately trust.

Remove unfamiliar numbers.

## Step 5. Check account details

Review:

- Your primary email address.
- Recovery information.
- Trusted devices.
- Trusted phone numbers.
- Payment methods.
- Account security settings.

Correct anything that has been changed without your permission.

## Step 6. Check your Apple services

Look for unfamiliar activity involving:

- iCloud Photos.
- iCloud Drive.
- Messages.
- Contacts.
- Find My.
- App Store purchases.
- Family Sharing.

If you see purchases you did not make, contact Apple Support.

## Step 7. Check for unwanted access

If someone may have had physical access to your iPhone or iPad, review:

- Face ID or Touch ID settings.
- Device passcode.
- Location sharing.
- Family Sharing.
- Installed applications.
- Profiles and device management settings.

Change your device passcode if someone else may know it.

## Step 8. Turn on two-factor authentication

Apple accounts use two-factor authentication to help protect sign-ins.

Review your trusted devices and phone numbers. Never share verification codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

## Step 9. If your device is lost or stolen

Use:

[Find My](https://www.icloud.com/find)

If you believe your device has been stolen, mark it as lost where possible and contact your mobile provider.

Do not remove a stolen device from Find My simply because someone messages you asking you to do so.

## If you cannot recover your account

Use:

[Apple Account Recovery](https://iforgot.apple.com/)

Follow Apple's instructions. Recovery may take time.
