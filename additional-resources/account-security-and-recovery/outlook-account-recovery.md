# Outlook & Microsoft Account Recovery

**Think someone has accessed your Outlook, Hotmail, Live or Microsoft account?**

Your Microsoft account may provide access to email, OneDrive, Xbox, Microsoft 365, saved passwords and other services.

Securing it quickly is important.

## Step 1. Try to recover your account

Use Microsoft's official recovery options:

- [Microsoft Sign-in Helper](https://aka.ms/sign-in-helper)
- [Microsoft Account Recovery Form](https://account.live.com/acsr)
- [Microsoft Account Security](https://account.microsoft.com/security)

If you can still sign in, go directly to your account security settings.

If you cannot sign in, follow Microsoft's recovery process. You may be asked to provide information that helps prove the account belongs to you.

## Step 2. Change your password

If you can access your account:

1. Open [Microsoft Account Security](https://account.microsoft.com/security).
2. Choose the password or security options.
3. Change your password.
4. Use a new password you have not used anywhere else.

If you cannot sign in, use the recovery process above.

## Step 3. Check recent activity

Visit:

[Microsoft Recent Activity](https://account.live.com/Activity)

Look for:

- Sign-ins from unfamiliar locations.
- Devices you do not recognise.
- Successful sign-ins you did not perform.
- Unusual account changes.
- Password or security information changes.

Follow Microsoft's prompts if you see activity that wasn't yours.

## Step 4. Check recovery information

Review your security information.

Check that:

- Your recovery email address is correct.
- Your phone number is correct.
- Your authentication methods belong to you.
- No unfamiliar security methods have been added.

Remove anything you do not recognise.

## Step 5. Sign out other sessions

Use the security settings to sign out everywhere if that option is available.

You may need to sign back in on your own devices afterwards.

## Step 6. Check Outlook settings

An attacker may have changed your mailbox settings to secretly receive copies of your email.

Open [Outlook on the web](https://outlook.live.com/).

Check:

### Forwarding

Open Outlook settings and look for mail forwarding.

Make sure messages are not being forwarded to an unfamiliar email address.

### Inbox rules

Review your mail rules.

Look for rules that:

- Forward messages.
- Delete security notifications.
- Move messages into unusual folders.
- Mark messages as read.
- Hide messages from Microsoft or financial institutions.

Remove anything you did not create.

### Automatic replies

Check whether an unfamiliar automatic reply has been configured.

### Sent and deleted messages

Look for messages you did not send and unexpected deleted items.

## Step 7. Check connected services

Review access to:

- OneDrive.
- Microsoft 365.
- Xbox.
- Microsoft Store purchases.
- Other services using your Microsoft account.

Look for unfamiliar activity, files, purchases or sharing permissions.

## Step 8. Turn on multi-factor authentication

Multi-factor authentication adds another security check when signing in.

Use an authenticator app, passkey or another supported method where possible.

Never approve a sign-in request you did not initiate.

## Step 9. Secure other accounts

If you reused your Microsoft password elsewhere, change those passwords too.

Start with your banking, other email accounts and important services.

## If you still cannot recover the account

Continue through Microsoft's official recovery process:

[Microsoft Sign-in Helper](https://aka.ms/sign-in-helper)

Be cautious of people offering paid account recovery services. Do not give them your password or verification codes.
