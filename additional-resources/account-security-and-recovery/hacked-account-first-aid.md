# Hacked Account First Aid

**Think someone has accessed one of your online accounts? Start here.**

It might be your email, social media, gaming account, shopping account or something else.

First: don't panic. There are practical steps you can take to limit the damage and regain control.

## 1. Work out what happened

Look for signs such as:

- Password reset emails you didn't request.
- Messages sent from your account that you didn't write.
- Purchases or transactions you don't recognise.
- New devices or sign-ins you don't recognise.
- Your password suddenly stops working.
- Friends or contacts receive strange messages from you.
- Your recovery email address or phone number has changed.

One strange notification doesn't automatically mean your account has been hacked. Check the account directly using its official app or website.

## 2. Secure your email account first

Your email account is often the key to resetting passwords for other services.

If someone controls your email, they may be able to reset your social media, shopping, gaming and other accounts.

If your email may be compromised:

1. Use a trusted device if possible.
2. Open your email provider's official website or app.
3. Change your password to something unique.
4. Check your recovery email address and phone number.
5. Remove devices and sessions you don't recognise.
6. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
7. Check for unfamiliar forwarding rules, filters and automatic replies.

Use the dedicated Outlook or Gmail guide in this folder if needed.

## 3. Recover the affected account

Use the service's official recovery process.

Do not use recovery links sent to you by strangers or people claiming they can get your account back.

Official recovery guides:

- [Microsoft / Outlook](https://account.live.com/acsr)
- [Google / Gmail](https://accounts.google.com/signin/recovery)
- [Apple](https://iforgot.apple.com/)
- [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/hacked)
- [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/hacked/)

## 4. Change your password

If you can still access the account:

1. Change the password.
2. Make it unique to that account.
3. Do not reuse an old password.
4. Use a password manager if you have one.

If you used the same password on other services, change those passwords too. Start with email, banking and important accounts.

## 5. Sign out other devices

Look for a setting such as:

- Sign out of all devices
- Where you're logged in
- Active sessions
- Devices
- Recent activity

End sessions you don't recognise. If possible, sign out all sessions and sign back in yourself.

## 6. Check account settings

Someone who has accessed your account may have changed settings to keep access.

Check:

- Recovery email addresses.
- Recovery phone numbers.
- Multi-factor authentication methods.
- Trusted devices.
- Email forwarding and filters.
- Connected apps and websites.
- Payment methods.
- Recent messages, posts and purchases.

Remove anything you don't recognise.

## 7. Protect your money

If you entered banking details, card details or payment information into a suspicious website, contact your bank immediately.

Use the number on your bank card or the bank's official website. Ask them to check for unauthorised transactions and secure your account.

If money has been transferred, report it immediately. Do not wait until you've finished recovering your online accounts.

## 8. Warn your contacts

If someone has been sending messages from your account, let your contacts know.

They should ignore unexpected requests for money, passwords, verification codes or suspicious links.

Contact people through another method if possible.

## 9. Check your devices

If you downloaded or ran a suspicious file, installed an unfamiliar app or believe your device is infected:

- Stop using the affected device for banking and password changes.
- Disconnect it from the internet if you suspect active malware.
- Run a scan using trusted security software.
- Seek help from a trusted IT professional if you are unsure.

Simply clicking a link does not automatically mean your device is infected. See [Clicked a Suspicious Link?](clicked-a-suspicious-link.md).

## 10. Report the incident

In Australia:

- [ReportCyber](https://www.cyber.gov.au/report) – report cybercrime.
- [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/) – report scams.
- [IDCARE](https://www.idcare.org/) – identity and cyber support, 1800 595 160.
- [ACSC Hotline](https://www.cyber.gov.au/) – 1300 292 371.

## Watch out for recovery scams

After an account is compromised, you may be contacted by someone claiming they can recover it.

Be careful of anyone who:

- Demands payment to recover an account.
- Asks for your password or verification code.
- Wants remote access to your device.
- Claims to have special contacts inside a platform.
- Promises guaranteed recovery.

Never share your password, one-time code or recovery code with another person.

## You're not alone

Account compromises happen to people every day. The important thing is to act quickly, secure the accounts that matter most and ask for help when needed.
