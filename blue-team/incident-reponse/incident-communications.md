# Incident Communications

## Overview

Incident communications ensure that the right people receive accurate, timely and appropriately controlled information during a security incident.

Poor communication can delay response, create confusion, expose sensitive information or result in unverified claims being distributed.

## Communication principles

- Use approved communication channels.
- Keep messages factual and concise.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions.
- Identify the intended audience.
- Share only information necessary for that audience.
- Record significant communications.
- Coordinate external statements through authorised personnel.
- Avoid speculation about attribution or impact.
- Protect sensitive technical and personal information.

## Communication roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Incident Commander | Coordinates incident status and key decisions |
| Technical Lead | Provides verified technical findings |
| Communications Lead | Coordinates approved messaging |
| Management | Approves business-level decisions and messaging |
| Legal / Privacy | Advises on legal and notification considerations |
| Service Owner | Communicates operational impact to relevant teams |

## Internal audiences

Depending on the incident, communication may be required for:

- IT and security teams.
- Executive management.
- Service and application owners.
- Finance.
- Human resources.
- Legal and privacy teams.
- Customer support.
- Employees.
- Business continuity teams.

## External audiences

Potential external stakeholders include:

- Customers.
- Suppliers.
- Business partners.
- Cloud and technology providers.
- Insurers.
- Regulators.
- Law enforcement.
- Specialist incident response providers.
- Media.

External communication must be approved and coordinated through the organisation's established process.

## Initial notification

An initial incident notification should include:

- Incident reference.
- Date and time detected.
- High-level description.
- Known operational impact.
- Immediate actions taken.
- Current severity.
- Required assistance.
- Next update time.
- Approved contact point.

Do not include unverified claims or unnecessary indicators that could expose the investigation.

## Status updates

A useful status update includes:

1. Current incident status.
2. Confirmed developments.
3. Affected services or users.
4. Actions completed.
5. Current risks.
6. Decisions required.
7. Next steps.
8. Next update time.

Use consistent timestamps and timezones.

## Employee communications

Employee messages may need to explain:

- What happened at a high level.
- Whether employees need to take action.
- Which systems or services are affected.
- How to report suspicious activity.
- Which communication channels remain trusted.
- Where to obtain support.

Do not ask employees to investigate suspected malware themselves or forward sensitive evidence through unapproved channels.

## Customer communications

Where customer communication is required, coordinate with legal, privacy, management and relevant business owners.

Communications should be accurate, appropriately scoped and consistent with applicable notification obligations.

Do not speculate about the nature or extent of data exposure.

## Communication security

During an incident, assume that normal communication channels may be compromised.

Consider:

- Separate approved incident channels.
- Restricted access to incident records.
- Verified contact details.
- Strong authentication.
- Out-of-band verification for sensitive instructions.
- Avoidance of compromised email accounts.
- Secure evidence transfer.

## Communication log

Maintain a record of:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Timestamp | Date and time sent |
| Sender | Author or approving role |
| Audience | Recipients or stakeholder group |
| Channel | Email, phone, incident platform or other |
| Purpose | Notification, update, request or decision |
| Summary | Brief description |
| Approval | Required approval reference |
| Follow-up | Next action or response |

## Common communication mistakes

- Sending unverified findings.
- Using compromised email accounts.
- Sharing detailed indicators with broad audiences.
- Making unauthorised statements to customers or media.
- Failing to record notification decisions.
- Using inconsistent incident terminology.
- Failing to communicate operational impacts.
- Promising a recovery time without sufficient evidence.
