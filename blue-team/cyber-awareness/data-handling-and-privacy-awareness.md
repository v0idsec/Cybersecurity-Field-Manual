# Data Handling and Privacy Awareness

## Overview

Organisations collect, process and store information that may be commercially sensitive, confidential or personal.

Employees must understand how to handle information appropriately throughout its lifecycle.

## Information Classification

An organisation may use classifications such as:

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| Public | Approved for unrestricted public distribution |
| Internal | Intended for ordinary internal business use |
| Confidential | Restricted to authorised individuals |
| Highly Restricted | Requires additional controls and explicit authorisation |

Always follow the organisation's actual classification scheme.

## Data Handling Principles

- Access information only when required for your role.
- Use approved storage locations.
- Share information only with authorised recipients.
- Apply appropriate permissions.
- Protect information during transmission.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication.
- Dispose of information securely.
- Report accidental disclosure.

## Email and File Sharing

Before sharing information:

- Confirm the recipient.
- Check the attachment.
- Verify the sharing permissions.
- Confirm whether external sharing is approved.
- Check that the information is appropriate for the recipient.
- Avoid using personal email or consumer file-sharing services for business data.

Pay particular attention to autocomplete when sending sensitive information.

## Cloud Storage

Use approved organisational storage platforms.

Employees should:

- Follow sharing policies.
- Avoid public links unless authorised.
- Review external collaborators.
- Remove access when no longer required.
- Avoid storing sensitive files in personal accounts.
- Follow retention requirements.

## Personal Information

Personal information may include:

- Names and contact details.
- Government identifiers.
- Financial information.
- Employment records.
- Health information.
- Customer records.
- Authentication information.

Some information may receive additional legal protection.

Handle personal information according to applicable privacy obligations and organisational policy.

## Data Minimisation

Only collect and retain information that is necessary for the business purpose.

Avoid:

- Collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Keeping duplicate records without a reason.
- Retaining information beyond approved periods.
- Sharing entire datasets when a smaller subset is sufficient.

## Printing and Physical Documents

Employees should:

- Collect print jobs promptly.
- Avoid leaving sensitive documents unattended.
- Use secure disposal facilities.
- Store documents in approved locations.
- Protect meeting notes and whiteboards.
- Follow clean-desk requirements.

## Accidental Disclosure

Examples:

- Sending a file to the wrong recipient.
- Sharing a public link unintentionally.
- Uploading information to an incorrect location.
- Leaving printed documents in a public area.
- Discussing confidential information in an inappropriate setting.

If disclosure occurs:

1. Report it immediately.
2. Do not attempt to conceal the incident.
3. Provide details of the information involved.
4. Identify known recipients and exposure.
5. Follow instructions from privacy, legal and security teams.
6. Preserve relevant evidence.

Do not independently delete evidence or contact affected individuals unless authorised.

## Secure Disposal

Follow approved disposal procedures for:

- Printed records.
- Removable storage.
- Retired devices.
- Backup media.
- Electronic files.
- Paper notes containing sensitive information.

Deleting a file does not necessarily mean the data has been securely destroyed.

## Privacy Responsibilities

Employees should understand:

- What information they handle.
- Why it is collected.
- Who may access it.
- How long it is retained.
- How to report a privacy incident.
- Where to seek privacy advice.

## Key Takeaways

Protect information according to its sensitivity, share only what is necessary and report accidental disclosure immediately.
