# Awareness Campaign Planning

## Overview

Security awareness campaigns focus attention on a particular risk, behaviour or security topic.

Campaigns should be relevant to current organisational risks and provide employees with clear actions they can take.

## Campaign Objectives

Examples:

- Increase reporting of suspicious emails.
- Improve MFA security.
- Reduce accidental data disclosure.
- Improve awareness of invoice fraud.
- Encourage secure remote working.
- Improve understanding of incident reporting.
- Reinforce physical security requirements.

## Campaign Lifecycle

### 1. Identify the Risk

Use:

- Incident reports.
- Threat intelligence.
- Vulnerability assessments.
- Employee feedback.
- Audit findings.
- Changes to business processes.
- Emerging attack techniques.

Select a topic that addresses a genuine organisational need.

### 2. Define the Audience

Identify the relevant employee groups.

Examples:

- All employees.
- Finance staff.
- Executives.
- IT administrators.
- Developers.
- Customer support.
- Remote workers.

### 3. Establish Objectives

Define what employees should know or do after the campaign.

Example:

- Employees will know how to verify supplier bank account changes using an independently established contact method.

### 4. Develop Campaign Content

Possible formats:

- Short articles.
- Internal emails.
- Posters.
- Videos.
- Quick-reference guides.
- Team presentations.
- Knowledge checks.
- Tabletop exercises.
- Authorised simulations.

### 5. Obtain Approval

Before publication, confirm:

- Campaign owner.
- Content accuracy.
- Communications approval.
- Legal or privacy review where required.
- Accessibility requirements.
- Delivery dates.
- Support arrangements.

Simulations and exercises require explicit written authorisation and defined scope.

### 6. Deliver the Campaign

- Publish through approved channels.
- Ensure employees can access materials.
- Provide a clear call to action.
- Make support available.
- Reinforce the message through managers.

### 7. Evaluate Results

Measure:

- Content engagement.
- Training completion.
- Knowledge assessment results.
- Reporting behaviour.
- Employee feedback.
- Relevant incident trends.

## Example Campaign: MFA Fatigue

**Objective:** Improve employee awareness of unexpected MFA requests.

**Audience:** All employees.

**Key messages:**

- Never approve an authentication request you did not initiate.
- Repeated prompts may indicate an attempted account compromise.
- Report unexpected authentication activity.
- Contact IT using an approved channel.

**Delivery:**

- Short internal article.
- Quick-reference guide.
- Team meeting reminder.
- Knowledge check.

**Success measures:**

- Improved knowledge assessment results.
- Increased reporting of unexpected prompts.
- Reduced approval of unsolicited authentication requests where measurable.

## Campaign Calendar

| Campaign | Suggested Frequency |
|---|---|
| Phishing awareness | Quarterly |
| Password and MFA | Twice yearly |
| Data handling | Twice yearly |
| Privacy awareness | Annually |
| Remote working | Annually |
| Incident reporting | Quarterly reinforcement |
| Physical security | Annually |
| Emerging threats | As required |

Frequency should reflect risk and employee feedback.

## Communication Guidelines

Campaign content should:

- Use plain language.
- Avoid unnecessary technical detail.
- Include realistic examples.
- Explain the expected behaviour.
- Provide a clear reporting path.
- Avoid fear-based messaging.
- Avoid blaming employees.

## Campaign Review

After delivery, document:

- What worked.
- What did not work.
- Employee feedback.
- Measurement results.
- Technical or communication issues.
- Recommended changes.
- Follow-up actions.

## Key Takeaways

A good campaign has a defined risk, a clear audience, a practical message and a measurable outcome.
