# Security Culture and Reporting

## Overview

Security culture describes the attitudes, behaviours and expectations that influence how people approach security in an organisation.

A positive security culture encourages employees to recognise risks, follow appropriate procedures and report suspicious activity without fear of embarrassment or unnecessary blame.

## Why Reporting Matters

Early reporting can help security teams:

- Identify active attacks.
- Contain compromised accounts.
- Block malicious infrastructure.
- Prevent additional data exposure.
- Identify patterns across multiple users.
- Preserve evidence.
- Reduce incident impact.

An employee reporting a suspicious email may provide the first indication of a wider campaign.

## Characteristics of a Positive Security Culture

- Reporting is encouraged.
- Employees know how to request help.
- Security responsibilities are clear.
- Management supports secure behaviour.
- Mistakes are treated as opportunities to improve.
- Policies are practical and understandable.
- Security teams communicate outcomes.
- Employees are not punished for reporting genuine mistakes in good faith.

## Establishing Reporting Channels

Provide simple, accessible reporting options.

Examples:

- Report phishing email button.
- Dedicated security mailbox.
- Service desk ticket category.
- Internal incident reporting portal.
- Emergency phone contact.
- Escalation to a manager or security representative.

The reporting process should be easy to locate.

## What Employees Should Report

- Suspicious emails or messages.
- Unexpected MFA prompts.
- Lost or stolen devices.
- Accidental disclosure of information.
- Unusual account activity.
- Suspected malware.
- Unauthorised access.
- Unexpected password reset notifications.
- Physical security concerns.
- Unusual requests for sensitive information.

Encourage reporting even when the employee is unsure whether an event is malicious.

## Recommended Reporting Process

1. Stop interacting with the suspicious content.
2. Do not forward suspicious attachments to colleagues.
3. Use the approved reporting channel.
4. Provide relevant details.
5. Follow instructions from IT or security personnel.
6. Do not attempt independent remediation unless instructed.

For suspected account compromise, employees should use a trusted device and approved contact method when following response instructions.

## Responding to Reports

Security teams should:

- Acknowledge reports promptly.
- Assess urgency.
- Provide clear next steps.
- Avoid blaming the reporter.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Escalate confirmed incidents.
- Share appropriate outcomes.
- Identify opportunities for wider education.

## Reporting Metrics

Useful measures include:

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Number of reports | Measures reporting activity |
| Time to report | Measures reporting speed |
| Time to triage | Measures security response |
| Valid report rate | Measures useful reporting |
| Duplicate report rate | Helps identify campaign activity |
| Repeat incident patterns | Identifies recurring weaknesses |
| Employee confidence | Measures reporting culture |

A high reporting rate may indicate improved awareness rather than increased exposure.

## Avoiding Blame

If an employee clicks a malicious link or makes a mistake:

- Focus on containing the incident.
- Preserve evidence.
- Establish what happened.
- Determine whether additional accounts or systems are affected.
- Identify contributing factors.
- Improve controls and processes.
- Provide supportive education.

Do not discourage reporting by publicly naming or shaming employees.

## Management Responsibilities

Managers should:

- Reinforce reporting expectations.
- Support attendance at relevant training.
- Avoid discouraging escalation.
- Make time for security activities.
- Support process improvements.
- Demonstrate secure behaviour.

## Key Takeaways

A successful reporting culture makes employees part of the organisation's early-warning system.

The objective is to make reporting easy, timely and constructive.
