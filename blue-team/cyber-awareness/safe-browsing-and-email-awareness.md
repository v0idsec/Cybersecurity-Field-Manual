# Safe Browsing and Email Awareness

## Overview

Email and web browsing are common entry points for cyber attacks.

Employees should be able to identify suspicious communications, recognise unsafe browsing behaviour and understand how to report potential threats.

## Email Threats

Common email-based threats include:

- Phishing.
- Spear phishing.
- Business email compromise.
- Malicious attachments.
- Credential theft.
- Invoice fraud.
- Supplier impersonation.
- Malicious links.
- Email account compromise.

## Recognising Suspicious Emails

Warning signs include:

- Unexpected requests for sensitive information.
- Urgent payment instructions.
- Unusual sender addresses.
- Unexpected attachments.
- Links that do not match the claimed destination.
- Requests to bypass normal approval processes.
- Unexpected password reset messages.
- Changes to supplier banking details.
- Unusual tone or writing patterns.

No single indicator proves an email is malicious. Assess the overall context.

## Sender Verification

Check:

- The full sender address.
- The display name.
- Whether the sender is expected.
- Whether the request is consistent with normal business processes.
- Whether the message is asking for unusual action.

Display names can be misleading.

## Links

Before opening an unexpected link:

- Check whether the destination is expected.
- Be cautious of shortened URLs.
- Look for misspelled or misleading domains.
- Avoid entering credentials through unsolicited links.
- Navigate directly to known services where possible.

HTTPS does not guarantee that a website is legitimate.

## Attachments

Treat unexpected attachments cautiously, especially:

- Executable files.
- Script files.
- Compressed archives.
- Office documents requesting macro activation.
- Files requiring unusual permissions.
- Password-protected archives from unexpected senders.

Do not disable endpoint protection or bypass security warnings to open a file.

## Business Email Compromise

Business email compromise may involve:

- Executive impersonation.
- Supplier account changes.
- Fake invoices.
- Fraudulent payment requests.
- Compromised business mailboxes.

Always verify sensitive financial changes using an independently established contact method.

Do not rely on phone numbers or contact details contained only in the suspicious message.

## Safe Browsing

Employees should:

- Use approved browsers.
- Keep browsers updated.
- Avoid untrusted downloads.
- Use approved extensions.
- Avoid installing unapproved software.
- Be cautious when granting website permissions.
- Avoid accessing sensitive systems from untrusted devices.
- Follow organisational web filtering requirements.

## Browser Warning Signs

Be cautious when websites:

- Request unnecessary permissions.
- Prompt unexpected software downloads.
- Display repeated fake security alerts.
- Request credentials unexpectedly.
- Redirect through unrelated domains.
- Claim an urgent device infection.
- Ask users to paste commands into system tools.

Never follow instructions to run unknown commands simply because a website claims they will fix an issue.

## Public Wi-Fi

When working away from the office:

- Prefer trusted networks.
- Avoid unknown or suspicious access points.
- Use approved VPN services where required.
- Avoid exposing sensitive information on shared devices.
- Confirm the legitimacy of networks before connecting.
- Follow organisational remote access policies.

## Reporting Suspicious Content

Employees should:

1. Stop interacting with the message or website.
2. Avoid forwarding malicious content to colleagues.
3. Use the approved reporting process.
4. Include relevant context.
5. Follow instructions from IT or security personnel.

If credentials were entered into a suspicious website, report this immediately.

## Key Takeaways

Treat unexpected requests carefully, verify sensitive actions independently and report suspicious communications.

A short verification step can prevent a significant incident.
