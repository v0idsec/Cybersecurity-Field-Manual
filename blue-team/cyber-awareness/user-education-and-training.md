# User Education and Training

## Overview

Security education helps employees develop the knowledge and practical skills required to perform their roles securely.

Training should be designed around real responsibilities rather than delivering the same technical content to every employee.

## Training Design Principles

Effective training should be:

- Role-specific.
- Relevant to actual business processes.
- Clear and accessible.
- Practical rather than purely theoretical.
- Delivered in manageable sections.
- Reinforced periodically.
- Evaluated for effectiveness.

## Training Categories

### General Employee Training

Topics:

- Phishing and social engineering.
- Passwords and MFA.
- Safe browsing.
- Data handling.
- Device security.
- Incident reporting.
- Physical security.

### Management Training

Topics:

- Business email compromise.
- Executive impersonation.
- Payment fraud.
- Data breach responsibilities.
- Incident escalation.
- Risk ownership.
- Crisis communication.

### Finance Training

Topics:

- Invoice fraud.
- Bank account change requests.
- Supplier impersonation.
- Payment verification.
- Urgent or unusual financial requests.

### Human Resources Training

Topics:

- Protection of employee records.
- Recruitment scams.
- Sensitive document handling.
- Identity verification.
- Secure onboarding and offboarding.

### IT and Security Training

Topics:

- Privileged access security.
- Credential protection.
- Secure administration.
- Incident escalation.
- Social engineering against support staff.
- Remote access security.
- Logging and monitoring responsibilities.

### Developer Training

Topics:

- Secure coding.
- Secrets management.
- Dependency security.
- Input validation.
- Authentication and authorisation.
- Secure code review.
- Vulnerability reporting.

## Training Delivery Methods

| Method | Advantages | Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Online modules | Scalable and repeatable | Can become passive |
| Instructor-led sessions | Interactive and adaptable | Requires scheduling |
| Workshops | Practical learning | Requires preparation |
| Quick-reference guides | Easy to revisit | Limited depth |
| Tabletop exercises | Tests decision-making | Requires facilitation |
| Simulations | Provides practical feedback | Requires approval and safeguards |
| Internal communications | Frequent reinforcement | Must avoid message fatigue |

## Knowledge Checks

Use short assessments to reinforce important concepts.

Example questions:

1. What should you do if you receive an unexpected MFA approval request?
2. How should an employee verify a supplier bank account change?
3. Where should suspicious emails be reported?
4. What should happen if sensitive information is sent to the wrong recipient?
5. Why should passwords not be reused?

Assess understanding rather than memorisation.

## Practical Exercises

Suitable exercises include:

- Identifying suspicious email indicators.
- Reviewing a fictional invoice fraud scenario.
- Selecting the correct data-sharing permissions.
- Reporting a simulated security incident.
- Identifying unsafe physical access behaviour.
- Practising verification of unusual requests.

Exercises should use fictional or approved test data.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

Training should consider:

- Different levels of technical knowledge.
- Language requirements.
- Accessibility needs.
- Screen-reader compatibility.
- Different learning preferences.
- Remote and shift-based employees.

Avoid assuming every employee has the same technical background.

## Training Records

Where required, record:

- Employee or audience identifier.
- Course name.
- Completion date.
- Assessment result.
- Training version.
- Required refresher date.
- Any follow-up actions.

Limit access to training records and retain them according to organisational policy.

## Evaluating Training

Consider:

- Did employees understand the material?
- Can they apply the knowledge?
- Are reporting rates improving?
- Are relevant incidents decreasing?
- Do employees know where to seek assistance?
- Is the content appropriate for the audience?

Training completion alone does not demonstrate effective learning.

## Key Takeaways

The best security training is practical, relevant and repeatable.

Employees should leave training knowing what to recognise, what action to take and where to get help.
