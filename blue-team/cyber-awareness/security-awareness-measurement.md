# Security Awareness Measurement

## Overview

Measuring security awareness helps determine whether education is improving employee knowledge, reporting behaviour and security outcomes.

A programme should not be judged solely by attendance or phishing simulation click rates.

## Measurement Objectives

Measurement should help answer:

- Are employees completing required training?
- Do employees understand key security concepts?
- Are suspicious events being reported?
- Are reports being submitted quickly?
- Are repeated mistakes decreasing?
- Are security behaviours improving?
- Are programme activities reducing organisational risk?

## Key Performance Indicators

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Training completion | Percentage of assigned training completed |
| Assessment performance | Knowledge assessment results |
| Phishing reporting rate | Percentage of simulated messages reported |
| Time to report | Time between delivery and reporting |
| Valid report rate | Percentage of reports assessed as relevant |
| Incident recurrence | Frequency of repeated incident types |
| Policy acknowledgement | Completion of required policy acknowledgements |
| Employee confidence | Self-reported confidence in security processes |

## Example Calculations

### Training Completion Rate

\[
\text{Completion Rate} =
\frac{\text{Completed Assignments}}{\text{Assigned Training}}
\times 100
\]

### Phishing Reporting Rate

\[
\text{Reporting Rate} =
\frac{\text{Users Who Reported}}{\text{Users Who Received the Simulation}}
\times 100
\]

### Simulation Click Rate

\[
\text{Click Rate} =
\frac{\text{Users Who Clicked}}{\text{Users Who Received the Simulation}}
\times 100
\]

Simulation results should be interpreted carefully. A click does not automatically demonstrate that an employee lacks security awareness.

## Leading and Lagging Indicators

### Leading Indicators

Measures that may indicate future improvements:

- Training participation.
- Knowledge assessment results.
- Reporting confidence.
- Phishing reporting rates.
- Policy acknowledgement.
- Participation in practical exercises.

### Lagging Indicators

Measures that reflect outcomes:

- Confirmed security incidents.
- Repeated incident categories.
- Account compromise incidents.
- Data disclosure incidents.
- Financial fraud incidents.
- Response and containment time.

## Baselines

Establish a baseline before evaluating programme improvements.

Document:

- Measurement period.
- Audience size.
- Data sources.
- Definitions.
- Exclusions.
- Known limitations.

Use consistent definitions between reporting periods.

## Trend Analysis

Review results over time.

Look for:

- Changes in reporting behaviour.
- Improvements in assessment scores.
- Departments requiring additional support.
- Recurring incident types.
- Changes after specific campaigns.
- Differences between employee groups.

Avoid interpreting small sample sizes as definitive evidence.

## Phishing Simulation Considerations

Authorised simulations can provide useful insight, but they should be designed carefully.

Requirements:

- Obtain written organisational approval.
- Define scope and objectives.
- Avoid collecting real passwords.
- Do not use actual sensitive information.
- Establish an appropriate support process.
- Protect individual-level results.
- Avoid public shaming.
- Provide constructive feedback.
- Define data retention requirements.

Measure reporting behaviour and learning outcomes, not only clicks.

## Reporting

A useful awareness report includes:

1. Reporting period.
2. Programme activities.
3. Audience coverage.
4. Completion rates.
5. Knowledge assessment results.
6. Reporting metrics.
7. Relevant incident trends.
8. Employee feedback.
9. Programme limitations.
10. Recommended improvements.

## Continuous Improvement

Use measurement results to:

- Update training content.
- Improve reporting channels.
- Target high-risk workflows.
- Address confusing policies.
- Provide additional support.
- Review technical controls.
- Adjust campaign frequency.

## Key Takeaways

Awareness measurement should demonstrate improvements in knowledge and behaviour.

Use a balanced set of metrics and avoid turning awareness programmes into employee scoreboards.
