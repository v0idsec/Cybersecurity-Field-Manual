# Threat Intelligence Lifecycle

## Overview

The threat intelligence lifecycle provides a repeatable process for turning raw information into useful security decisions.

## Lifecycle

1. Direction
2. Collection
3. Processing
4. Analysis
5. Dissemination
6. Feedback

## 1. Direction

Define what the organisation needs to understand.

Examples:

- Threats targeting the organisation's industry.
- Active exploitation of technologies in use.
- Campaigns targeting employees.
- Adversary techniques relevant to the environment.

Document intelligence requirements and priorities.

## 2. Collection

Gather relevant information from approved sources.

Examples:

- Government advisories.
- Vendor security reports.
- Threat intelligence platforms.
- Internal SIEM alerts.
- Incident response findings.
- Vulnerability disclosures.
- Trusted information-sharing communities.

Collection should be guided by intelligence requirements.

## 3. Processing

Convert collected information into a usable format.

Activities include:

- Normalising indicator formats.
- Removing duplicates.
- Extracting relevant artefacts.
- Recording timestamps.
- Identifying source information.
- Applying confidence and reliability ratings.
- Filtering irrelevant data.

## 4. Analysis

Determine what the information means.

Consider:

- Is the source trustworthy?
- Is the information current?
- Is the activity relevant?
- Can the findings be independently corroborated?
- Does the information match internal telemetry?
- What action should the organisation take?

Document assumptions and uncertainty.

## 5. Dissemination

Deliver findings to the appropriate teams.

Examples:

| Audience | Intelligence Use |
|---|---|
| SOC | Alert triage and detection |
| Incident Response | Investigation and containment |
| Vulnerability Management | Exploitation prioritisation |
| IT Operations | Security control updates |
| Management | Business risk decisions |

Use clear summaries and actionable recommendations.

## 6. Feedback

Evaluate whether intelligence was useful.

Ask:

- Did it answer the original requirement?
- Was the information accurate?
- Did it support a decision?
- Was it delivered in time?
- Should the collection requirements change?

## Continuous Improvement

The lifecycle is iterative.

New incidents, threat campaigns and business changes should influence future intelligence requirements.

## Key Takeaways

Threat intelligence is an ongoing process.

The value comes from turning relevant information into decisions and measurable security improvements.
