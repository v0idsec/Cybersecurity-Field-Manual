# Physical Reconnaissance

A reference for gathering information about a facility, its physical layout, security arrangements and publicly observable exposure before an authorised physical security assessment.

Reconnaissance should establish an understanding of the target environment without trespassing, interfering with operations or collecting unnecessary personal information.

## Objectives

- Understand the facility's physical footprint.
- Identify publicly visible entrances and access points.
- Understand the surrounding environment.
- Identify publicly documented security arrangements.
- Establish likely assessment priorities.
- Identify relevant third-party facilities and dependencies.
- Prepare an evidence-based assessment plan.

## 1. Public information gathering

Begin with information that the organisation has made publicly available.

Potential sources include:

- Organisation websites.
- Public property listings.
- Government planning records.
- Public building directories.
- Publicly available site maps.
- Public photographs.
- Company announcements.
- Job advertisements.
- Publicly accessible compliance documentation.

Record the source and date of each observation.

## 2. Mapping and geographic research

Mapping services can help establish the physical context of a facility.

Useful resources:

| Resource | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Google Maps | Street maps and publicly visible locations |
| Google Earth | Satellite and historical imagery where available |
| OpenStreetMap | Geographic and infrastructure information |
| Nearmap | Commercial aerial imagery, subject to access |
| State and local planning portals | Public planning and development information |

Consider:

- Site boundaries.
- Adjacent properties.
- Public roads.
- Nearby transport infrastructure.
- Publicly visible entrances.
- Building layout.
- Publicly documented construction or renovation.

Imagery may be outdated. Treat it as an investigative lead rather than proof of current conditions.

## 3. Facility identification

Identify relevant facility characteristics.

Examples:

- Office buildings.
- Warehouses.
- Data centres.
- Retail locations.
- Distribution facilities.
- Industrial sites.
- Telecommunications facilities.
- Shared commercial premises.

Consider whether the organisation occupies an entire building or shares access and infrastructure with other tenants.

## 4. Publicly observable access points

Where observation is explicitly authorised, document visible access arrangements.

Examples:

- Main reception.
- Public entrances.
- Visitor entrances.
- Loading docks.
- Vehicle access points.
- Car parks.
- External emergency exits.
- Service entrances.

Record the presence of security controls without attempting to bypass them.

## 5. Public-facing security information

Look for publicly disclosed information about:

- Visitor registration.
- Security policies.
- Facility operating hours.
- Employee access procedures.
- Contractor management.
- Security responsibilities.
- Emergency arrangements.

Avoid targeting individual employees or collecting personal details that are not necessary for the assessment.

## 6. Personnel and organisational context

Relevant organisational information may help identify security responsibilities.

Sources include:

- Public job advertisements.
- Publicly listed security roles.
- Organisation structure pages.
- Public policy documents.
- Vendor and facilities-management information.

Focus on roles, responsibilities and processes rather than building dossiers on individual staff members.

## 7. Third-party dependencies

Facilities may rely on external organisations for:

- Security guards.
- Cleaning.
- Building management.
- Maintenance.
- Waste management.
- Telecommunications.
- Power and utilities.
- Visitor management.
- Access-control administration.

Identify relevant dependencies and determine whether they are included in the authorised assessment.

## 8. Reconnaissance documentation

Maintain a structured record.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Observation ID | Unique reference |
| Date | Date observed |
| Source | Information source |
| Location | Relevant facility or area |
| Observation | What was identified |
| Confidence | High, medium or low |
| Relevance | Why it matters |
| Follow-up | Additional validation required |

## 9. Limitations

Public information may be:

- Inaccurate.
- Outdated.
- Incomplete.
- Misleading.
- Unrepresentative of current operations.

Never present an unverified assumption as a confirmed security weakness.

## 10. Operational considerations

- Remain in publicly accessible areas unless authorised otherwise.
- Do not trespass.
- Do not interfere with staff or visitors.
- Do not photograph sensitive areas without permission.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal information.
- Do not test access controls during reconnaissance unless explicitly approved.
- Do not publish sensitive facility information.

## Reporting considerations

Reconnaissance observations should be used to guide assessment planning.

Where relevant, report:

- Publicly exposed facility information.
- Unclear access boundaries.
- Inconsistent visitor information.
- Third-party dependencies.
- Information that could increase physical security risk.

Separate informational observations from confirmed vulnerabilities.
