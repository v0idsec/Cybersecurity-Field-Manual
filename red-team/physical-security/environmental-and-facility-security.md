# Environmental and Facility Security

A reference for assessing the physical protection of critical infrastructure, sensitive facilities, equipment and supporting environmental systems.

Physical security extends beyond doors and access cards. Power, cooling, fire protection, water, cabling and building management systems can all affect the availability and security of an organisation's technology.

## Assessment objectives

- Evaluate protection of critical infrastructure.
- Review server room security.
- Assess environmental monitoring.
- Review power and cooling arrangements.
- Evaluate fire and water protection.
- Review telecommunications and cabling security.
- Assess facility maintenance and resilience.

## 1. Critical facility identification

Identify facilities and infrastructure whose compromise could affect:

- Confidentiality.
- Integrity.
- Availability.
- Business continuity.
- Physical safety.
- Regulatory compliance.

Examples:

- Server rooms.
- Data centres.
- Network distribution rooms.
- Telecommunications facilities.
- Electrical distribution rooms.
- Backup storage facilities.
- Building management control rooms.

## 2. Server room security

Review:

- Physical access restrictions.
- Access approval procedures.
- Visitor and contractor access.
- Entry logging.
- CCTV coverage.
- Environmental monitoring.
- Rack and cabinet security.
- Equipment inventories.
- Maintenance arrangements.

Determine whether access is limited to personnel with a legitimate operational requirement.

## 3. Network infrastructure

Assess the physical protection of:

- Network switches.
- Routers.
- Firewalls.
- Patch panels.
- Fibre distribution.
- Structured cabling.
- Wireless infrastructure.
- Telecommunications equipment.

Review whether network infrastructure is located in appropriately restricted areas.

## 4. Cabling security

Physical cabling can expose sensitive communications and infrastructure.

Consider:

- Cable routes.
- Distribution frames.
- Network outlets.
- External cable access.
- Unused ports.
- Cabinet security.
- Labelling practices.
- Maintenance access.

Assess whether cabling is protected against accidental damage and unauthorised access.

## 5. Power protection

Review:

- Uninterruptible power supplies.
- Backup generators.
- Power distribution units.
- Electrical redundancy.
- Maintenance schedules.
- Power monitoring.
- Fuel arrangements.
- Emergency shutdown procedures.

Confirm that critical systems have power protection appropriate to their availability requirements.

Do not operate or test electrical equipment without the appropriate qualified personnel and authorisation.

## 6. Cooling and environmental controls

Assess:

- Cooling capacity.
- Temperature monitoring.
- Humidity monitoring.
- Airflow management.
- Environmental alerting.
- Maintenance schedules.
- Redundancy.
- Equipment placement.

Review whether environmental thresholds are documented and monitored.

## 7. Fire protection

Review the design and maintenance of fire protection systems.

Examples:

- Smoke detection.
- Fire alarms.
- Fire suppression.
- Fire extinguishers.
- Fire-rated doors.
- Emergency exits.
- Evacuation signage.
- Inspection records.

Fire and life-safety systems must only be assessed through approved procedures involving qualified personnel.

## 8. Water and other environmental risks

Consider exposure to:

- Water leaks.
- Flooding.
- Storm damage.
- Excessive heat.
- Dust.
- Chemical exposure.
- Structural damage.
- Pest activity.

Review whether critical infrastructure is appropriately located and protected.

## 9. Building management systems

Building management systems may control:

- Heating.
- Ventilation.
- Air conditioning.
- Lighting.
- Power monitoring.
- Access systems.
- Environmental sensors.

Review:

- Physical access.
- Administrative access.
- Network connectivity.
- Vendor access.
- Change management.
- Logging.
- Maintenance.
- Backup and recovery.

Treat building management systems as operational technology where appropriate.

## 10. Backup and recovery facilities

Review the physical protection of:

- Backup media.
- Offline storage.
- Recovery equipment.
- Disaster recovery facilities.
- Critical documentation.
- Hardware spares.

Consider whether backup assets are exposed to the same physical risks as production systems.

## 11. Third-party maintenance

Review access granted to:

- Electrical contractors.
- HVAC technicians.
- Telecommunications providers.
- Cleaning personnel.
- Equipment vendors.
- Facilities-management providers.

Consider approval, supervision, access expiry and work records.

## 12. Controlled validation

Potential validation activities include:

- Reviewing environmental monitoring records.
- Inspecting approved infrastructure areas.
- Reviewing maintenance documentation.
- Confirming access restrictions.
- Reviewing alarm and notification records.
- Verifying equipment inventories.

Do not trigger outages, alter environmental controls or interfere with critical infrastructure.

## Common findings

| Finding | Potential impact |
|---|---|
| Unrestricted server room access | Increased risk of equipment compromise |
| Poor cabinet security | Unauthorised access to network infrastructure |
| Unprotected cabling | Exposure of communications infrastructure |
| Inadequate environmental monitoring | Delayed detection of damaging conditions |
| Shared power dependencies | Increased outage risk |
| Poor maintenance records | Reduced assurance of system reliability |
| Unsecured building management systems | Increased operational technology exposure |

## Remediation

- Restrict access to critical infrastructure.
- Maintain accurate equipment inventories.
- Secure network cabinets and distribution rooms.
- Review cabling routes and protection.
- Improve environmental monitoring.
- Maintain power and cooling systems.
- Review fire protection arrangements.
- Formalise third-party maintenance procedures.
- Test business continuity and recovery arrangements.
