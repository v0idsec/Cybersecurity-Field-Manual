# Perimeter and Site Security

A reference for assessing the physical boundaries and external protections surrounding an organisation's facilities.

Perimeter security establishes the first layer of physical protection. Its purpose is to deter, detect and delay unauthorised access while maintaining safe access for authorised personnel and emergency services.

## Assessment objectives

- Evaluate physical boundary protection.
- Review vehicle and pedestrian access arrangements.
- Identify publicly exposed areas.
- Assess external lighting and visibility.
- Review security signage.
- Examine maintenance and inspection processes.
- Evaluate integration with access-control and surveillance systems.

## 1. Boundary protection

Review the physical structures defining the facility boundary.

Examples:

- Fences.
- Walls.
- Gates.
- Bollards.
- Vehicle barriers.
- Landscaping.
- Retaining structures.
- External building walls.

Consider:

- Suitability for the site's risk profile.
- Physical condition.
- Maintenance history.
- Clear boundary definition.
- Integration with other security controls.
- Potential safety hazards.

## 2. Pedestrian access

Review designated pedestrian entrances.

Consider:

- Main entrances.
- Staff entrances.
- Visitor entrances.
- Contractor entrances.
- Emergency exits.
- Shared building access.
- Reception arrangements.

Evaluate whether access routes are clearly defined and appropriately controlled.

## 3. Vehicle access

Review vehicle entry and exit arrangements.

Examples:

- Vehicle gates.
- Boom gates.
- Intercom systems.
- Guarded vehicle entrances.
- Delivery access.
- Loading docks.
- Car park access controls.

Consider:

- Visitor and delivery procedures.
- Vehicle authorisation.
- Access logging.
- Tailored controls for restricted areas.
- Emergency vehicle access.
- Safety and traffic management.

## 4. External doors and windows

Inspect approved areas for:

- Door condition.
- Window condition.
- Locking hardware.
- Access-control readers.
- Door-position monitoring.
- Emergency exit arrangements.
- Visible damage or maintenance issues.

Do not attempt forced entry or physical manipulation of doors and windows.

## 5. Loading docks and service areas

Service areas can have different security requirements from public entrances.

Review:

- Delivery registration.
- Contractor identification.
- Delivery documentation.
- Restricted-area separation.
- Access logging.
- Goods receiving procedures.
- After-hours access arrangements.

Consider whether deliveries can be received without providing unnecessary access to the rest of the facility.

## 6. External lighting

Lighting contributes to visibility, deterrence and safety.

Assess:

- Entrance lighting.
- Car park lighting.
- Walkways.
- Loading areas.
- Building perimeter.
- Emergency access routes.
- Security camera illumination.

Consider whether lighting is suitable for operating hours and environmental conditions.

## 7. Signage and boundary identification

Review whether signs clearly identify:

- Public areas.
- Restricted areas.
- Visitor entrances.
- Staff-only areas.
- Emergency exits.
- Safety hazards.
- Site rules.

Poor signage can cause confusion and weaken access procedures.

## 8. Landscaping and environmental design

Review whether landscaping supports or undermines security.

Consider:

- Visibility around entrances.
- Overgrown vegetation.
- Obstructed cameras.
- Concealed maintenance areas.
- Pedestrian movement.
- Vehicle visibility.
- Emergency access.

Security improvements should not introduce unacceptable accessibility or safety issues.

## 9. Shared facilities

For multi-tenant buildings, determine:

- Who controls the external perimeter.
- Who manages common areas.
- How tenants receive access.
- How visitors are processed.
- Who maintains access-control systems.
- How security incidents are escalated.

Document responsibilities between the building owner, facilities manager and tenant.

## 10. Validation and evidence

Use approved, non-destructive assessment methods.

Evidence may include:

- Photographs of authorised areas.
- Site diagrams.
- Maintenance records.
- Access-control documentation.
- Inspection records.
- Interviews.
- Observed control deficiencies.

Do not create real security incidents to test perimeter response.

## Common findings

| Finding | Potential impact |
|---|---|
| Poorly maintained boundary | Reduced physical deterrence |
| Unclear visitor entrance | Inconsistent visitor handling |
| Inadequate external lighting | Reduced visibility and safety |
| Uncontrolled service access | Increased exposure of restricted areas |
| Poorly defined shared access | Unclear security responsibilities |
| Inadequate inspection procedures | Physical defects may remain undetected |

## Remediation considerations

Recommendations may include:

- Repairing damaged boundary structures.
- Improving entrance signage.
- Reviewing vehicle access procedures.
- Improving external lighting.
- Separating visitor and staff access.
- Introducing documented inspection schedules.
- Clarifying shared-facility responsibilities.
- Integrating perimeter controls with monitoring systems.
