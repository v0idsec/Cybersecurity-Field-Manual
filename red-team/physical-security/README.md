# Physical Security

A professional reference for assessing the physical security controls protecting people, facilities, equipment, infrastructure and sensitive information.

Physical security assessments examine whether an organisation's physical protections are appropriately designed, implemented, monitored and maintained.

## Assessment objectives

Physical security assessments may evaluate:

- Perimeter protection and site boundaries.
- Visitor management and reception procedures.
- Employee and contractor access controls.
- Badge, key and credential management.
- Mechanical and electronic locking systems.
- CCTV coverage, recording and monitoring.
- Intrusion detection and alarm response.
- Server rooms and restricted facilities.
- Protection of sensitive equipment and information.
- Security procedures, policies and staff awareness.
- Physical access risks affecting information systems.

## Assessment principles

All physical security testing must be explicitly authorised.

Before testing:

1. Obtain written permission from the asset owner.
2. Define the locations, buildings and systems in scope.
3. Agree on permitted and prohibited activities.
4. Establish testing hours and operational constraints.
5. Identify emergency contacts and escalation procedures.
6. Agree on safety requirements and evidence handling.
7. Establish stop conditions for unexpected access or safety concerns.

Never assume that permission to assess one building, floor or facility extends to other locations.

## Safety and operational considerations

Physical assessments can create risks that do not exist in conventional technical testing.

Do not:

- Force doors, windows or access-control systems.
- Damage locks, alarms, cameras or other equipment.
- Enter restricted areas outside the agreed scope.
- Interfere with emergency exits or life-safety systems.
- Handle hazardous materials or restricted infrastructure.
- Photograph people, documents or sensitive areas without permission.
- Attempt to obtain unauthorised access to personal property.
- Leave doors, gates or security systems in an insecure state.

Use approved test credentials, designated test areas and controlled scenarios wherever possible.

## Assessment lifecycle

1. Planning and authorisation.
2. Open-source and site reconnaissance.
3. Perimeter and facility assessment.
4. Access-control assessment.
5. Surveillance and monitoring assessment.
6. Environmental and infrastructure assessment.
7. Controlled validation.
8. Evidence collection.
9. Risk assessment and reporting.
10. Remediation and retesting.
