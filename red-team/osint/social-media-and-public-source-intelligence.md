# Social Media and Public Source Intelligence

## Overview

Social media and public-source intelligence uses publicly available online content to understand an organisation's public presence, professional relationships and security-relevant exposure.

Sources may include:
- Professional networking platforms.
- Public social media accounts.
- Company blogs.
- Public forums.
- Conference presentations.
- Public repositories.
- News articles.
- Community discussions.

This guide focuses on legitimate security research and organisational intelligence.

## 1. Public Social Media Research

Where authorised, review publicly available organisational accounts and professional content.

Potential intelligence includes:
- Official company accounts.
- Public announcements.
- Technology references.
- Product launches.
- Business relationships.
- Publicly documented personnel roles.
- Security-related announcements.

Do not attempt to access private accounts or bypass platform access controls.

## 2. Professional Networking Platforms

Professional profiles may provide useful organisational context.

Relevant public information:
- Job titles.
- Departments.
- Public professional experience.
- Publicly listed technical skills.
- Public company affiliations.
- Conference and publication history.

Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

## 3. Username and Identity Correlation

Public usernames may appear across multiple platforms.

Username correlation can help identify:
- Public developer profiles.
- Professional portfolios.
- Public code repositories.
- Forum accounts.
- Public technical contributions.

A matching username does not establish that two accounts belong to the same individual.

Use independent evidence before making attribution claims.

## 4. Public Forums and Communities

Relevant sources may include:
- Technical forums.
- Developer communities.
- Public discussion boards.
- Product support communities.
- Security research communities.
- Public Q&A platforms.

Potential observations:
- Public technology discussions.
- Support issues.
- Published configuration examples.
- Product usage.
- Publicly disclosed incidents.
- Technical documentation.

Do not impersonate users or attempt to access restricted communities.

## 5. Public Content Analysis

Review content for security-relevant information such as:
- Accidental disclosure of internal project details.
- Public references to infrastructure.
- Technology adoption.
- Publicly disclosed incidents.
- Misconfigured public links.
- Unintended exposure of documents.

Treat statements as contextual evidence rather than proof of technical configuration.

## 6. Image and Document Context

Public images and documents may contain metadata or contextual information.

Potential indicators:
- Document creation dates.
- Software versions.
- Author fields.
- Public location information.
- Organisation names.
- Technology references.

Metadata is covered in more detail in the broader OSINT methodology and tool references.

## 7. Correlation and Validation

When correlating public identities or accounts:

1. Identify the original source.
2. Record the exact public identifier.
3. Compare independent public evidence.
4. Establish whether the relationship is current.
5. Document alternative explanations.
6. Assign a confidence level.
7. Avoid unsupported personal attribution.

## 8. Reporting

Report only information relevant to the assessment.

Useful fields:
- Platform.
- Public account or source URL.
- Observation.
- Collection date.
- Relevance.
- Confidence.
- Supporting evidence.
- Limitations.

Avoid creating unnecessary personnel dossiers or including personal information that does not support a security finding.

## Privacy and Operational Considerations

- Obtain approval for personnel-focused research.
- Do not use deception or impersonation without explicit written authorisation.
- Do not contact individuals unless approved.
- Do not attempt to access private content.
- Do not bypass platform restrictions.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal information.
- Respect platform terms and applicable privacy requirements.
- Preserve only the evidence required for the engagement.
