# Vishing and Smishing

## Overview

Vishing (voice phishing) and smishing (SMS phishing) use telephone calls and text messages to influence recipients into taking actions or disclosing information.

Assessments should evaluate how employees verify unexpected requests and report suspicious communications.

## 1. Vishing

Vishing assessments use authorised phone-based scenarios.

Potential assessment objectives:
- Help desk identity verification.
- Unexpected IT support requests.
- Supplier verification.
- Executive request validation.
- Reporting of suspicious calls.
- Handling of requests for sensitive information.

## 2. Smishing

Smishing assessments evaluate responses to suspicious SMS messages.

Common themes:
- Delivery notifications.
- Account security alerts.
- Password or MFA notifications.
- Payroll updates.
- Appointment notifications.
- Supplier or business communications.

Use approved simulation numbers and avoid impersonating real individuals or unrelated organisations.

## 3. Common Warning Signs

### Voice Calls
- Unexpected requests for credentials.
- Pressure to act immediately.
- Requests to bypass normal procedures.
- Unverified claims of authority.
- Requests to install remote access software.
- Requests to approve unexpected MFA prompts.
- Refusal to use established verification channels.

### SMS
- Unexpected links.
- Shortened URLs.
- Unfamiliar sender numbers.
- Unexpected payment requests.
- Urgent account warnings.
- Requests for authentication codes.
- Inconsistent sender identity.

## 4. Help Desk Assessment

Help desk workflows are particularly important because staff may be asked to reset passwords or change account details.

Assess:
- Identity verification.
- Callback procedures.
- Approval requirements.
- Ticket validation.
- Manager escalation.
- Password reset controls.
- MFA reset controls.
- Audit trail quality.

Do not perform real password resets or change account recovery information unless specifically authorised.

## 5. Safe Call Scenarios

Suitable controlled scenarios include:
- A caller asks where to find the official support portal.
- A simulated employee requests information that should require identity verification.
- A caller asks for an exception to an established procedure.
- A caller claims to have an urgent but unverified request.

The interaction should stop before real credentials, account access or sensitive information are disclosed.

## 6. Safe SMS Simulations

Use:
- An approved sender identity.
- An organisation-controlled destination.
- A controlled landing page.
- Minimal event tracking.
- A clear debrief process.

Do not collect passwords, MFA codes, payment details or personal information.

## 7. Measurement

Useful metrics:
- Calls answered.
- Verification completed.
- Requests escalated.
- Sensitive information disclosed.
- Suspicious messages reported.
- Time to report.
- Procedures followed.

A call being answered or a message being opened does not establish a security failure.

## 8. Operational Safeguards

Before testing:
- Obtain written approval.
- Confirm approved phone numbers.
- Confirm target population.
- Confirm call and message limits.
- Exclude emergency and critical service numbers.
- Establish a stop contact.
- Coordinate with internal communications and security teams.

Never spoof emergency services or use caller identities that could cause emergency response or public confusion.

## 9. Reporting

Include:
- Scenario.
- Channel.
- Target population.
- Verification requirements.
- Observed behaviour.
- Reporting outcomes.
- Process weaknesses.
- Recommended improvements.
