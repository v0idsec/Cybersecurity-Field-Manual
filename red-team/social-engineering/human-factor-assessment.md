# Human Factor Assessment

## Overview

Human-factor assessments examine how organisational behaviour, processes and workplace culture influence security outcomes.

The objective is to identify where security depends too heavily on individual judgement, informal processes or assumptions of trust.

## 1. Assessment Areas

| Area | What to assess |
|---|---|
| Trust | Whether unfamiliar requests are challenged |
| Authority | Whether approval procedures are followed |
| Urgency | Whether time pressure causes controls to be bypassed |
| Information disclosure | Whether sensitive information is shared unnecessarily |
| Verification | Whether identities and requests are independently confirmed |
| Reporting | Whether suspicious activity is escalated |
| Security culture | Whether employees feel supported when raising concerns |
| Process consistency | Whether procedures are followed across teams |

## 2. Information Disclosure

Assess whether employees understand which information may be shared externally.

Examples:
- Internal contact directories.
- Organisational structure.
- Project names.
- Technology platforms.
- Supplier relationships.
- Internal procedures.
- Non-public operational information.

Use public information and approved test scenarios wherever possible.

Do not solicit personal, financial, medical or other sensitive information.

## 3. Elicitation

Elicitation is the process of obtaining information through conversation.

An assessment may evaluate whether employees:
- Confirm the identity of unexpected callers.
- Avoid disclosing internal information unnecessarily.
- Refer requests to approved channels.
- Recognise unusual requests.
- Follow confidentiality procedures.

Do not use deceptive conversations to obtain secrets, passwords or sensitive personal information.

## 4. Authority and Trust

Review whether staff can appropriately challenge requests that appear to come from:
- Management.
- IT support.
- Finance.
- Human resources.
- Suppliers.
- Contractors.
- External service providers.

A healthy security culture allows employees to verify unusual requests without fear of criticism.

## 5. Process Weaknesses

Common weaknesses include:
- Informal approval processes.
- Shared accounts.
- Unverified phone requests.
- Uncontrolled visitor access.
- Unclear reporting procedures.
- Inconsistent supplier verification.
- Excessive reliance on familiarity.
- Lack of escalation guidance.

## 6. Employee Reporting

Evaluate:
- Awareness of reporting channels.
- Ease of reporting.
- Confidence in the reporting process.
- Response times.
- Feedback to employees.
- Escalation procedures.

Reporting should be easy, accessible and supported by management.

## 7. Assessment Methods

Suitable methods include:
- Approved phishing simulations.
- Controlled phone scenarios.
- Visitor process observations.
- Policy and procedure reviews.
- Structured interviews.
- Anonymous surveys.
- Tabletop exercises.
- Help desk process reviews.

Use the least intrusive method that can answer the assessment question.

## 8. Privacy and Ethics

Protect employee privacy.

- Collect only necessary information.
- Prefer aggregated results.
- Restrict access to individual-level observations.
- Avoid public ranking or shaming.
- Do not record conversations without approval.
- Follow applicable workplace and privacy requirements.
- Define evidence retention periods.
- Provide an appropriate debrief.

## 9. Measuring Maturity

An example maturity scale:

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 — Ad hoc | Security relies on individual judgement |
| 2 — Developing | Basic procedures exist but are inconsistent |
| 3 — Defined | Procedures are documented and communicated |
| 4 — Managed | Compliance is measured and reviewed |
| 5 — Improving | Controls are continuously evaluated and improved |

Assess each business process independently rather than assigning one simplistic score to the entire organisation.

## 10. Reporting

Report:
- Assessment objectives.
- Methods used.
- Process weaknesses.
- Organisational impact.
- Positive behaviours.
- Training opportunities.
- Recommended procedural improvements.
- Limitations.

The report should help the organisation improve its processes and culture rather than blame employees for individual decisions.
