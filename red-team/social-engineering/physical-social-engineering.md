# Physical Social Engineering

## Overview

Physical social engineering assessments evaluate whether an organisation's physical security procedures can prevent unauthorised access.

The focus is on people, processes and access controls — not bypassing safety systems or creating physical danger.

All physical testing requires explicit written approval identifying the permitted buildings, dates, methods and boundaries.

## 1. Assessment Objectives

Evaluate:
- Visitor management.
- Reception procedures.
- Contractor verification.
- Badge and access card controls.
- Restricted area enforcement.
- Staff challenge behaviour.
- Security desk procedures.
- Lost badge handling.
- Physical incident reporting.

## 2. Scope and Safety

Before testing, document:
- Approved buildings and entrances.
- Approved testing times.
- Permitted interaction types.
- Areas explicitly excluded.
- Whether staff interaction is permitted.
- Whether access-control testing is permitted.
- Emergency contacts.
- Security team notification arrangements.
- Stop conditions.

Never enter:
- Residential areas.
- Medical treatment areas.
- Emergency response facilities.
- Safety-critical operational areas.
- Restricted government facilities.
- Any location outside the written scope.

Do not interfere with locks, alarms, fire systems, CCTV, emergency exits or life-safety equipment.

## 3. Visitor Management

Review:
- Visitor registration.
- Identity verification.
- Host confirmation.
- Visitor badges.
- Escort requirements.
- Visitor sign-out.
- After-hours access.
- Contractor procedures.

Assess whether the organisation can identify and account for visitors.

## 4. Tailgating Assessment

Tailgating occurs when an unauthorised person enters a controlled area by following an authorised person.

A controlled assessment should evaluate whether staff:
- Challenge unfamiliar individuals.
- Prevent unauthorised entry.
- Avoid holding secure doors open for unknown visitors.
- Report suspicious access behaviour.
- Follow visitor escort procedures.

Do not force doors, obstruct access or physically pressure employees.

## 5. Impersonation Scenarios

Approved scenarios may evaluate whether staff verify:
- Contractors.
- Delivery personnel.
- Maintenance workers.
- Visitors.
- External support personnel.

Use controlled identities and stop before entering areas or accessing systems beyond the approved boundary.

Do not impersonate emergency personnel, law enforcement or actual government officials.

## 6. Badge and Access Card Controls

Review:
- Badge issuance.
- Badge expiry.
- Lost or stolen badge handling.
- Access revocation.
- Visitor badge restrictions.
- Access review processes.
- Shared badge practices.
- After-hours access.

Do not clone, alter or use real access credentials without explicit approval and a defined test procedure.

## 7. Restricted Areas

Identify whether access restrictions are:
- Clearly marked.
- Enforced by access controls.
- Supported by staff procedures.
- Monitored.
- Reviewed regularly.

Potential restricted areas include:
- Server rooms.
- Network equipment rooms.
- Records storage.
- Finance offices.
- Security operations areas.
- Equipment storage.

Any test involving access to these areas must be separately authorised.

## 8. Physical Evidence

Use appropriate evidence:
- Time and location.
- Approved observer notes.
- Access-control event references.
- Visitor process observations.
- Photographs where expressly authorised.

Do not photograph employees, screens, documents or sensitive areas without approval.

Do not remove physical assets or documents.

## 9. Incident and Stop Conditions

Stop the assessment if:
- Security staff initiate an emergency response.
- A participant becomes distressed.
- The activity disrupts business operations.
- A safety risk emerges.
- Testing enters an excluded area.
- A real security incident occurs.
- The authorising contact requests suspension.

Follow the organisation's emergency and incident procedures.

## 10. Reporting

Document:
- Location.
- Assessment scenario.
- Relevant physical control.
- Observed process.
- Whether access was challenged.
- Evidence.
- Potential impact.
- Remediation.

Avoid identifying individual employees unless necessary and explicitly authorised.
