# Social Engineering

## Overview

Social engineering assessments evaluate how people, processes and technical controls respond to attempts to manipulate trust, authority, urgency or routine business activity.

The objective is to identify weaknesses in organisational processes and improve resilience — not to embarrass employees or collect unnecessary personal information.

This section covers authorised human-factor assessments, including phishing simulations, pretexting, vishing, smishing and physical social engineering.

## Assessment Types

- Email phishing simulations.
- Targeted phishing simulations.
- Business email compromise simulations.
- Voice phishing (vishing).
- SMS phishing (smishing).
- Physical access-control assessments.
- Visitor and contractor process assessments.
- Information disclosure assessments.
- Help desk identity-verification assessments.
- Security awareness and reporting effectiveness assessments.

## Engagement Principles

1. Obtain written authorisation before testing.
2. Define permitted scenarios and target populations.
3. Establish clear exclusions and stop conditions.
4. Minimise collection of personal information.
5. Never collect real passwords or MFA codes.
6. Do not access personal accounts or devices.
7. Avoid scenarios likely to cause distress or reputational harm.
8. Coordinate with emergency, security and incident-response teams.
9. Preserve evidence securely.
10. Report organisational weaknesses constructively.

## Important Boundaries

Social engineering can affect employees, customers, suppliers and the public.

Explicit approval is required for activities such as:
- Impersonating executives or external organisations.
- Contacting customers or third parties.
- Testing help desk identity-verification procedures.
- Sending SMS messages or making calls.
- Physical entry attempts.
- Testing access badges or visitor procedures.
- Using real brand identities or domains.
- Any interaction that could trigger financial transactions or operational changes.

A phishing simulation should use a controlled landing page and record only the minimum event data needed to evaluate the exercise.

## Assessment Outcomes

A useful assessment should determine:
- Whether employees recognise suspicious activity.
- Whether reporting mechanisms are accessible.
- Whether identity verification procedures are followed.
- Whether technical controls reduce exposure.
- Whether suspicious activity reaches the appropriate team.
- Whether the organisation can respond effectively.

The objective is measurable improvement, not simply a click rate.
